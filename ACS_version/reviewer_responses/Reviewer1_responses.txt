COMMENT 2:
What is the evidence for the choice of Co, Fe and Ni rather than other
transition metals?
----------------------------------------------------------------

RESPONSE 2:
The selection of Co, Fe, and Ni is motivated by three considerations,
now made explicit in the Introduction (highlighted in blue):

(i) Electronic structure: Co (d7), Fe (d6), and Ni (d8) are late 3d
    metals with partially occupied d-shells that hybridize with the
    Cr-Cl framework and place them near the thermoneutral HER window
    on the volcano plot. Early metals (Ti, V) strongly overbind H;
    late metals (Cu, Zn) have filled/near-filled d-shells with poor
    hybridization.

(ii) Magnetic compatibility: CrCl3 hosts a Cr3+ (d3) Heisenberg
     ferromagnet. Co2+, Fe2+/3+, and Ni2+ carry 1-4 uB magnetic
     moments, making them natural candidates for spin-coordination-
     catalysis studies in magnetic 2D hosts.

(iii) Experimental realizability: Co, Fe, and Ni are the most commonly
      reported TM functionalization agents for CrCl3 and related
      chromium trihalides in both theory and experiment
      [Luo2020, Gao2018, Mastrippolito2021, Abdel-Hafiez2025].

This choice is also consistent with recent HER catalyst studies
(Moraes et al., IJHE 2026, 212, 153692), which similarly focus on
Co, Fe, and Ni as benchmark 3d active sites near the HER optimum.

COMMENT 3:
The authors should not only consider the doping model of alloy elements
in CrCl3, but also the structural model of alloy elements in hydrogen
adsorption on CrCl3.
----------------------------------------------------------------

RESPONSE 3:
This is precisely the central design strategy of the paper. We
explicitly consider two structural coordination motifs for each metal:

(a) Surface-adsorbed: TM above the basal plane in threefold hollow
    coordination (~3 Cl neighbors). Direct H-binding access.

(b) Embedded (intercalated): TM penetrates the Cl-terminated surface
    with ~6 Cl neighbors from both Cl layers. Reduced site exposure.

H adsorption is studied in all six configurations (3 metals x 2 motifs),
fully relaxing H/TM/CrCl3. Results are reported in Figure 7 and
Section 3.4. The coordination motif is shown to be the decisive variable.

We have added a sentence in the Introduction (blue) to make this
dual-configuration strategy more prominent.

COMMENT 5:
Which surface of CrCl3 did the author select for hydrogen adsorption?
What was the basis for this selection? If dG_ads > 0, can it still
serve as a hydrogen storage material?
----------------------------------------------------------------

RESPONSE 5:
(a) Surface: The (001) basal plane and the Cl-terminated surface is exposed
    by mechanical exfoliation or vapor-phase growth. Also for a free-standing
    2D monolayer, this is the only accessible face. The mentioned its consistent with the
    2D CrCl3 literature (Webster2018, Mastrippolito2021, Zala2024).

(b) Hydrogen storage vs. HER: This work targets HER electrocatalysis,
    not hydrogen storage. Considering the ideal HER catalyst has dG_ads ~ 0. 
    A positive dG_ads (endergonic adsorption relative to 1/2 H2) means 
    the material is a poor HER catalyst, not a storage material. Then the pristine 
    CrCl3 (dG_ads = 1.82 eV) is thermodynamically far from the HER optimum. 
    TM functionalization reduces this to 0.20 eV (Co), 0.51 eV (Fe), 
    0.88 eV (Ni).

We have added explicit HER/storage clarification in the Introduction
and in Section 3.1 (highlighted in blue).

----------------------------------------------------------------
COMMENT 6:
Is the author considering CrCl3 as a hydrogen storage material or as a
hydrogen evolution catalyst? What is its hydrogen storage upper limit?
----------------------------------------------------------------

RESPONSE 6:
TM-functionalized CrCl3 is studied here exclusively as a model HER
electrocatalyst. This is established by:
  1. Use of the CHE framework optimizing toward dG_ads ~ 0 (not
     strongly negative as needed for storage).
  2. Single-site surface coverage (1 H per 2x2x1 supercell).
  3. No investigation of intercalation capacity or wt% storage.

Questions of hydrogen storage capacity and commercial potential are
outside the scope of this work. Explicit clarification has been added to
the Introduction and Figure 7 discussion (highlighted in blue).
