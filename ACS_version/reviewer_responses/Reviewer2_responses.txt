COMMENT 1:
Spin-orbit coupling (SOC) was ignored. How will SOC modify TM magnetic
moments, orbital hybridization and hydrogen adsorption free energy across
adsorbed and embedded configurations?
----------------------------------------------------------------

RESPONSE 1:
We thank the Reviewer for raising this important methodological point.

SOC effects in the CrCl3 family depend on the atomic number
of the halide ligand. In CrI3, the heavy iodine (Z=53) generates strong SOC that determines the Ising-like magnetic anisotropy. Because CrCl3 contains chlorine (Z=17), a light ligand, SOC is substantially weaker.
Also the established consensus in the 2D CrCl3 literature is that CrCl3 is better described by a Heisenberg (or XY-like) model rather than the Ising model of CrI3, precisely because the weaker SOC from Cl does not
dominate the exchange anisotropy like the papers in Soriano2020, Webster2018, etc.

For the specific quantities investigated in this work:

(A) TM MAGNETIC MOMENTS: The local magnetic moments calculated in this study are exchange-dominated spin moments determined by the d-electron count and spin-polarized hybridization with Cl-3p and Cr-3d states. The calculated values are: Surface-adsorbed: Co = 1.78 uB, Fe = 2.72 uB, Ni = -0.76 uB; Embedded:         Co = 2.08 uB, Fe = 3.30 uB, Ni = -1.02 uB
These are spin moments. SOC would add an orbital component (~0.05-0.15 uB for 3d metals in this coordination) but would not change the spin-moment ordering or the qualitative trend. For 3d transition metals (Co, Fe, Ni), the orbital moment is quenched by the crystal field; any SOC-induced correction to the total moment is typically <5% and does not alter the bonding conclusions.

(B) ORBITAL HYBRIDIZATION / ELECTRONIC STRUCTURE: SOC would split degenerate d-orbital manifolds by tens of meV for 3d metals in this coordination. The TM-Cl ICOHP bond strengths (Co: -6.70 eV cumulative 3-fold; Fe: -6.84 eV; Ni: -6.18 eV for adsorbed) are dominated by exchange and crystal-field effects at the scale of eV, making a meV-scale SOC splitting a secondary correction.

(C) HYDROGEN ADSORPTION FREE ENERGY: The DeltaG_ads values (Co: +0.20 eV, Fe: +0.51 eV, Ni: +0.88 eV for surface-adsorbed) are determined by total-energy differences on the scale of 0.1-1 eV. SOC corrections to DeltaG_ads in 3d-metal systems have been shown to be <20 meV in comparable systems (e.g., Fe/Co on graphene, MoS2 single-atom catalysts), well below the level that would change the qualitative conclusions or the metal ranking.

The systematic study of Feng et al. (IJHE 2021, 46, 5378) on 3d metal catalysts similarly does not include SOC, consistent with standard practice for light-ligand 3d magnetic catalysts.

We have added a paragraph in the Computational Methods section (highlighted in blue in the marked manuscript) explicitly discussing the SOC approximation, its justification for the CrCl3+Co/Fe/Ni system, and the estimated magnitude of the correction, with citations
to the relevant literature on SOC in chromium trihalides.

For the embedded configurations (DeltaG_ads >> 1 eV), coverage effects would not change the conclusion of their poor activity. COMMENT 3:
This work only evaluates H adsorption thermodynamics without reaction
barriers. Can you supply LST/QST transition state energies of Volmer
and Tafel steps to fully compare HER kinetics?
----------------------------------------------------------------

RESPONSE 3:
The calculation of Volmer (H+ + e- -> H*) and Tafel (2H* -> H2) or Heyrovsky (H* + H+ + e- -> H2) transition states requires nudged elastic band (NEB) calculations, ideally with the VASP-VTST package and explicit treatment of the electrochemical interface (e.g., via the double-reference method or implicit solvation). These are computationally demanding calculations that go well beyond the scope of the present work, which is explicitly a thermodynamic descriptor study within the CHE framework.

We note that within the CHE approach adopted in this work: The theoretical overpotential eta = |DeltaG_ads|/e provides a lower bound on the kinetic barrier for the Volmer step under the assumption that the reaction is adsorption-limited. For surface-adsorbed Co: eta = 0.20 V -- among the lowest computed for any 3d-functionalized 2D magnetic system. This framework is standard in the HER computational catalysis literature (Norskov2005, Moraes2026, Feng2021, FENG202236294).

Single-atom/adatom systems like those studied here typically show low Tafel barriers because the active site geometry favors direct Langmuir-Hinshelwood H+H recombination. The kinetic bottleneck is generally the Volmer adsorption step, which is well-captured
by DeltaG_ads.

We have added a sentence in the Conclusions section (highlighted blue) explicitly stating that this work provides thermodynamic descriptors within the CHE framework and that full kinetic analysis (NEB/transition states) is an important direction for future work.

(A) WHAT 5 ps AT 300 K ESTABLISHES: Our AIMD trajectories confirm that Co and Fe remain predominantly surface-exposed at 300 K without reconstructing the CrCl3 lattice or desorbing within 5 ps (Figure 3g-h). (B) LIMITATIONS ACKNOWLEDGED: We explicitly state in the manuscript (Section 3.3): "The trajectories reveal short-time structural accessibility but do not determine long-time diffusion kinetics, incorporation barriers, or equilibrium populations of the two coordination motifs." This is an honest assessment of what the AIMD provides.

COMMENT 5:
You never compare CrCl3 with CrBr3/CrI3. How do heavier halide ligands
alter TM-Cl bonding strength and optimize H adsorption for the same
threefold Co active sites?
----------------------------------------------------------------

RESPONSE 5:
The comparison with CrBr3/CrI3 is an excellent direction for future
work. The present study focuses on CrCl3 as a well-defined reference
platform for the following reasons:

(A) SCOPE: CrCl3 is the most computationally tractable member of
    the CrX3 family for this class of study. Including CrBr3/CrI3
    would require a separate study with recalibrated pseudopotentials,
    U parameters, and COHP projectors for heavier halides.

(B) HALIDE TREND (QUALITATIVE): The expected trend across the
    CrX3 family (X = Cl, Br, I) is:
    - Stronger TM-X bonds for heavier halogens due to larger
      orbital overlap and more diffuse p-states.
    - Larger crystal-field splitting from more polarizable halide
      ligands, which would modify TM-3d level positions.
    - Stronger SOC in CrI3 would add a non-collinear component
      absent in CrCl3.
    - Our ICOHP analysis shows that the key descriptor is the
      cumulative TM-Cl coordination (adsorbed threefold: ~-6.7 eV
      for Co; embedded sixfold: ~-11.2 eV), which directly controls
      active-site exposure. In CrBr3/CrI3, larger lattice constants
      and more diffuse ligand orbitals might reduce ICOHP magnitudes,
      potentially increasing site exposure for the same TM.

(C) EXPERIMENTAL CONTEXT: CrCl3 has better ambient stability than
    CrI3 (which degrades under ambient conditions), making it the
    more experimentally relevant platform for electrocatalysis.

We have added a sentence in the Conclusions (highlighted blue)
acknowledging the CrBr3/CrI3 comparison as a natural extension.

COMMENT 6:
The d-band center shows weak linear correlation with DeltaG_ads. What
combined electronic descriptor integrating spin splitting and ICOHP
values can better predict hydrogen binding strength?
----------------------------------------------------------------

RESPONSE 6:
We fully agree with this assessment, and it is explicitly discussed in
Section 3.5 of the manuscript. The weak R^2 (0.362 for surface-adsorbed;
0.770 for embedded) of the d-band center correlation is attributed to:

(A) SPIN-POLARIZED d-MANIFOLD: A spin-summed first moment collapses
    the spin-up and spin-down channels, losing the information about
    spin-polarization-induced orbital asymmetry that directly
    governs H-3d hybridization. For systems with large local moments
    (Fe: 2.72 uB; Co: 1.78 uB; Ni: -0.76 uB), the spin-resolved
    d-band centers differ significantly between channels.

(B) ICOHP AS A COMPLEMENTARY DESCRIPTOR: The cumulative TM-Cl ICOHP
    captures the bonding strength of TM center to its coordination
    shell -- directly related to the orbital availability for H
    binding. Our data show:
      Adsorbed:  Co ICOHP_TM-Cl = -6.70 eV, DeltaG = +0.20 eV (best)
                 Fe ICOHP_TM-Cl = -6.84 eV, DeltaG = +0.51 eV
                 Ni ICOHP_TM-Cl = -6.18 eV, DeltaG = +0.88 eV
      Embedded:  Co ICOHP_TM-Cl = -11.22 eV, DeltaG = +1.74 eV
                 Fe ICOHP_TM-Cl = -11.40 eV, DeltaG = +1.33 eV
                 Ni ICOHP_TM-Cl = -10.01 eV, DeltaG = +1.89 eV
    The pattern shows that higher cumulative ICOHP (stronger TM-Cl
    bonding in embedded configs) correlates with more unfavorable
    DeltaG_ads, consistent with the coordination-controlled trade-off.

(C) COMBINED DESCRIPTOR PROPOSAL: A combined descriptor of the form:
    Xi = epsilon_d^occ(spin-up) - epsilon_d^occ(spin-dn) + alpha*ICOHP_TM-Cl
    where the first term captures spin-splitting and the second
    captures coordination bonding, would be a more complete predictor.
    With only three metals per coordination family, fitting such a
    multi-parameter descriptor is not statistically meaningful, and
    we have deliberately avoided over-parametrizing the analysis.

We have added a brief paragraph in Section 3.5 (highlighted blue)
explicitly proposing the spin-splitting + ICOHP combined descriptor
as a direction for future multi-system studies, referencing the
ICOHP data above.

COMMENT 7:
No direct benchmark against Pt or single-atom M-N-C was provided.
Can you compute their DeltaG_ads to quantify Co-CrCl3's advantages?
----------------------------------------------------------------

RESPONSE 7:
We appreciate the suggestion. The well-established benchmark values
from the literature are:
  Pt(111):         DeltaG_ads ~ -0.09 eV (slightly too strong binding)
  Pt single-atom:  DeltaG_ads ~ -0.03 to +0.08 eV
  Co-N4 (M-N-C):  DeltaG_ads ~ -0.10 to +0.15 eV depending on N-C geometry
  Fe-N4 (M-N-C):  DeltaG_ads ~ -0.47 eV (too strong)
  Ni-N4 (M-N-C):  DeltaG_ads ~ +0.35 eV
  [Norskov2005, Feng2021 (Cu1@GDY: +0.18 eV limit)]

Surface-adsorbed Co on CrCl3 (DeltaG_ads = +0.20 eV) is:
  - Comparable to Co-N4 M-N-C catalysts (+0.10 to +0.15 eV)
  - Slightly less optimal than Pt(111) (-0.09 eV) but in the
    same near-thermoneutral zone (|DeltaG_ads| < 0.3 eV)
  - Superior to Fe-N4 and Ni-based single-atom systems

Recalculating Pt and M-N-C under identical DFT parameters (same
functional, pseudopotentials, supercell approach) would require
dedicated calculations that are beyond the scope of this manuscript
and would constitute a separate benchmarking study. However, the
literature values cited above provide the relevant context.

We have added a brief comparison paragraph in Section 3.4
(highlighted blue) citing these literature DeltaG_ads values for
Pt and M-N-C benchmarks (Norskov2005, Feng2021), explicitly
contextualizing Co-CrCl3 within the broader HER landscape.

COMMENT 8:
Monolayer CrCl3 suffers from high raw material and manufacturing costs,
restricting large-scale commercial H2 production. Low-cost carbon or
metal oxide catalysts are more suitable for industrial mass production.
----------------------------------------------------------------

RESPONSE 8:
We acknowledge the Reviewer's practical concern. This work is an
exploratory computational study of the fundamental physicochemical
principles governing H adsorption on a 2D magnetic platform, not a
proposal for industrial deployment. In this context:

(A) RESEARCH SCOPE: As with all first-principles studies of novel 2D
    platforms (including the referenced work by Feng et al. on
    graphdiyne-supported Cu (IJHE 2021) and biphenylene (IJHE 2022)),
    the primary contribution is mechanistic understanding: how
    coordination engineering, spin-polarization, and metal-ligand
    bonding jointly determine HER activity descriptors. This
    understanding can guide the design of cost-effective analogs.

(B) COST PERSPECTIVE: CrCl3 can be synthesized from abundant
    precursors (Cr metal + Cl2 or HCl) via chemical vapor transport.
    The material is commercially available and used in organic
    synthesis (as a Lewis acid catalyst). Monolayer fabrication by
    mechanical exfoliation or CVD is feasible at laboratory scale,
    as demonstrated experimentally (Mastrippolito2021, Abdel-Hafiez2025).
    The claim that "high manufacturing costs restrict large-scale
    production" applies equally to graphdiyne (Feng2021), biphenylene
    (Feng2022), and essentially any 2D material beyond graphene -- yet
    these materials are widely studied as proof-of-concept platforms.

(C) PRACTICAL RELEVANCE: The coordination engineering principles
    identified here (threefold vs. sixfold coordination; active-site
    exposure as a design rule) are transferable to more cost-effective
    systems, including TM-doped carbon materials, MXenes, and 2D
    chalcogenides. We have added a sentence in the Conclusions making
    this broader transferability explicit (highlighted blue).
