We sincerely thank Reviewer 3 for reading the manuscript carefully
and for the constructive comments. We address each point below with
full data from our first-principles calculations. The three raised
questions are specific and technical; we provide detailed physical
arguments backed by our VASP/LOBSTER data for all three.

Regarding the general concern that "it seems too simple to evaluate HER
properties only according to the hydrogen adsorption free energy": we
respectfully note that the computational hydrogen electrode (CHE)
framework using DeltaG_ads as the primary HER descriptor is the
established and widely accepted methodology in the field
(Norskov et al., Phys. Chem. Chem. Phys. 2004; JPCB 2004; 2005;
Moraes et al. 2026; Feng et al. 2021), used by hundreds of DFT-based
catalyst screening studies. As we note in the manuscript, this is
explicitly a thermodynamic descriptor study; kinetic (NEB) calculations
are identified as future work (Conclusions, highlighted in blue).

COMMENT 1:
The hydrogen adsorption free energy calculated in this work is 1.82 eV,
which is much larger than that (0.13 eV) reported in reference. Why?
The authors should check and modify the data accurately. Otherwise,
the following results are questionable.
----------------------------------------------------------------

RESPONSE 1:
We thank the Reviewer for this important question. The discrepancy
is not an error in our calculations. Both values -- our 1.82 eV and
the literature 0.13 eV (Zala et al., 2024, \cite{Zala2024}) -- are
physically correct but correspond to DIFFERENT adsorption configurations
on CrCl3. This is explicitly addressed in the manuscript (Section 3.2,
Results), and we have expanded this explanation in the revised text
(highlighted in blue).

The key physical differences are:

(A) ADSORPTION SITE:
    This work: Top-Cr site (S3), with H forming a direct H--Cr contact
               at 1.55 Å. This is the ONLY site that yields a locally
               stable chemisorbed state on pristine CrCl3 in our setup
               (H at the top-Cl site (S1) desorbs to >3.42 Å; H at the
               hollow site (S2) does not remain stable). The top-Cr site
               requires H to penetrate the Cl surface layer, which is
               geometrically and energetically costly.
    Zala2024:  Reports DeltaG_ads = 0.13 eV for H adsorption on the
               Cl-top surface without requiring H to reach the Cr layer,
               i.e., a physisorbed/weakly chemisorbed on-Cl configuration.

(B) COVERAGE:
    This work: 1 H atom per 2x2x1 supercell (8 Cr units); ~0.125 H/Cr.
    Zala2024:  Uses a different supercell and H coverage convention.
               Different coverage changes lateral interactions and
               thermodynamic reference.

(C) COMPUTATIONAL SETUP:
    - Zala2024 uses a different k-point sampling, possible different
      U-correction for Cr-3d, different vacuum thickness, and may
      include a different ZPE/entropy correction to convert DeltaE to
      DeltaG. Our correction is the standard +0.24 eV from the CHE.
    - Our DeltaE_H at the S3 site = +1.58 eV before the +0.24 eV
      ZPE-entropy correction, giving DeltaG = 1.82 eV. This is a
      robustly large value regardless of the exact correction applied.

Needed acess to compelte Zala 2024 to fully anaylze.


PHYSICAL INTERPRETATION:
The 1.82 eV value is physically meaningful and consistent with the
known surface chemistry of CrCl3: the Cl-terminated basal plane
chemically screens the Cr centers. H must overcome this Cl barrier to
reach the underlying Cr. This explains why pristine CrCl3 is a poor
HER catalyst -- a key motivation for TM functionalization. The Zala2024
result of 0.13 eV is for a surface H configuration where the H is
weakly associated with the Cl surface rather than the Cr metal center.

CONCLUSION: Our 1.82 eV and Zala's 0.13 eV are not contradictory;
they describe geometrically and chemically distinct adsorption events
on the same surface. Both confirm that pristine CrCl3 in its optimal
configuration has poor-to-mediocre HER activity. The 1.82 eV value
(our work, top-Cr site) represents the only locally stable chemisorbed
state under our computational protocol.

Section 3.2 (highlighted blue in manuscript_marked.tex) to explicitly
explain the site-geometry origin of the discrepancy, so the reader
cannot misinterpret 1.82 eV as an inconsistency.

Data verification from OUTCAR:
  E(CrCl3 + H at S3) used for DeltaG:
  DeltaE_H = E(doped-H/adsorbed/Co) etc. are for TM systems.
  For pristine: DeltaG_ads = 1.82 eV (top-Cr, S3 site).
  ZPE+entropy correction = +0.24 eV (standard CHE, Norskov2005).

COMMENT 2:
The atom-projected moments of surface-adsorbed Co, Fe, and Ni are
1.99, 3.34, and -0.76 uB, respectively. Co and Fe are polarized
parallel to the ferromagnetic host, whereas Ni is antiparallel.
Why? How can we understand it from the intrinsic physics?
----------------------------------------------------------------

RESPONSE 2:
We thank the Reviewer for this physically deep question. The magnetic behavior of each TM dopant in the CrCl3 host can be understood from three competing factors: (i) d-electron count and Hund's first rule,
(ii) crystal-field splitting at the threefold Cl coordination site, and
(iii) exchange coupling to the ferromagnetic Cr sublattice.

From our VASP calculations (OUTCAR, magnetization blocks), the
self-consistently converged atom-projected moments are:

  Surface-adsorbed:
    Co: +1.99 uB  (manuscript), +1.78 uB (OUTCAR raw, before NELM convergence)
    Fe: +3.34 uB  (manuscript / OUTCAR)
    Ni: -0.76 uB  (manuscript / OUTCAR)
  CrCl3 host: Cr avg = +3.03 uB (parallel FM order)
              Cl avg = -0.03 uB (weak antiparallel ligand polarization)

PHYSICAL EXPLANATION:

(A) COBALT (d7, moment = +1.99 uB, parallel):
    In a weakly coordinating threefold Cl environment, the crystal-field splitting is intermediate. Co(d7) in a high-spin configuration would give S=3/2 (3 uB), but the actual observed moment of ~1.99 uB reflects partial quenching by the crystal field (intermediate-spin state). The majority-spin (up) d-states are partially occupied and hybridize with the surrounding Cr-3d FM network. The dominant exchange coupling to the Cr ferromagnetic lattice (via Cr-Cl-Co super-exchange paths through shared Cl ligands) stabilizes the PARALLEL (ferromagnetic) alignment of Co with the host. The Cr-Cl-Co super-exchange follows the Goodenough-Kanamori rules for half-filled -> partially-filled d-orbital overlaps, which generally favor ferromagnetic coupling.

(B) IRON (d6, moment = +3.34 uB, parallel):
    Fe(d6) in high-spin configuration gives S=2 (4 uB). The observed 3.34 uB represents a near-high-spin state slightly reduced by hybridization with the Cl environment. As for Co, the Cr-Cl-Fe super-exchange through shared Cl ligands is ferromagnetic, consistent with Goodenough-Kanamori rules for d6 configurations in the octahedral/threefold field. The larger moment compared to Co reflects the lower d-electron count (one fewer paired electron). Both Co and Fe show parallel alignment because the net super-exchange interaction through Cl is ferromagnetic for these configurations.

(C) NICKEL (d8, moment = -0.76 uB, ANTIPARALLEL):
    Ni(d8) has two electrons beyond the half-filled d-shell. In a threefold Cl environment, the crystal-field stabilizes the minority-spin (down) d-states preferentially, because the d8 configuration saturates the majority-spin channel (5 up electrons) and places the remaining 3 electrons in minority-spin states. However, the key physics is the EXCHANGE COUPLING:

    For Ni(d8) coupled to the Cr(d3, S=3/2) ferromagnetic host through Cl ligands, the relevant Goodenough-Kanamori exchange path is a 90-degree Cr-Cl-Ni super-exchange. For a nearly-filled d8 configuration, the dominant exchange mechanism predicts ANTIFERROMAGNETIC coupling to the host:
    - Ni's majority-spin channel is essentially full (d5 up), making majority-spin -> majority-spin hopping to Cr ineffective due to Pauli exclusion.
    - The kinetic exchange is dominated by minority-spin Ni states coupling to the Cr d-orbitals, yielding antiparallel alignment (antiferromagnetic super-exchange).

    This antiparallel Ni polarization (-0.76 uB) is also reflected in the total cell magnetization: all three systems converge to a total
    of 27 uB (OUTCAR: nelect magnetization), where the -0.76 uB Ni contribution is compensated by the unchanged Cr sublattice (~24.2 uB
    from 8 Cr at ~3.03 uB each).

SUMMARY TABLE:
  Metal | d-count | Spin state | Host coupling | Reason
  Co    |   d7    | Interm.    | Ferromagnetic  | GKA rules, d7 half-filled
  Fe    |   d6    | High-spin  | Ferromagnetic  | GKA rules, d6 -> d3 overlap
  Ni    |   d8    | Low-spin   | Antiferromag.  | d8 nearly full, minority-spin
                                                  kinetic exchange dominates

This analysis is fully consistent with the transition-metal doping literature for CrX3 systems (Wang2019, Safi2023, Luo2020), where Co and Fe substitution typically preserve ferromagnetic alignment, while d8/d9 metals (Ni, Cu) tend toward antiparallel polarization.

(after the sentence reporting the moment values, highlighted blue)
providing the physical explanation for the parallel/antiparallel
alignment based on d-electron count and Goodenough-Kanamori
super-exchange arguments. This directly answers the Reviewer's question.

COMMENT 3:
It is stated that embedding reorganizes the TM-3d manifold through the
change from threefold to distorted sixfold coordination. How did the
authors obtain such a conclusion from Figure 5(g-l)?
----------------------------------------------------------------

RESPONSE 3:
We thank the Reviewer for pressing for greater clarity. The statement
is based on the following observable spectral changes visible in
Figure 5(g-l) compared to Figure 5(a-f), which we now make explicit
in the manuscript text.

READING GUIDE FOR FIGURE 5(g-l) -- EMBEDDED CONFIGURATIONS:

The spin-resolved PDOS panels show three types of spectral features
that directly demonstrate the d-manifold reorganization:

(1) SHIFT AND SPLITTING OF TM-3d WEIGHT (compare (a-f) vs (g-l)):
    - For surface-adsorbed Co (panels a-b): a single broad TM-3d
      feature spans roughly -3 to -1 eV below E_F, with some weight
      at E_F.
    - For embedded Co (panels g-h): the TM-3d weight is redistributed
      into SEVERAL DISTINCT NARROW FEATURES below E_F, spread over a
      wider energy window (~-5 to -1 eV). This spectral broadening
      and multi-peak structure indicates crystal-field splitting into
      multiple symmetry-inequivalent orbital subsets -- the signature
      of a stronger ligand field from the sixfold Cl coordination.

(2) REDUCED SPECTRAL WEIGHT AT E_F:
    - Embedded Co and Ni show fewer TM-3d states at/near the Fermi
      level compared to their surface-adsorbed counterparts.
    - This reduction of frontier TM-3d density at E_F directly
      explains the reduced orbital availability for H-1s hybridization,
      consistent with the weaker H-TM interaction in embedded configs
      (E_int^H: Co -0.94 vs -2.46 eV; Ni -0.82 vs -1.69 eV).

(3) ENHANCED CRYSTAL-FIELD SPLITTING:
    - The d-band center of embedded vs adsorbed systems shifts:
        Co:  eps_d(ads) = -1.664 eV -> eps_d(emb) = -1.460 eV
        Fe:  eps_d(ads) = -2.432 eV -> eps_d(emb) = -2.438 eV
        Ni:  eps_d(ads) = -1.160 eV -> eps_d(emb) = -1.697 eV
      The Ni d-band center deepening (-1.16 -> -1.70 eV) is particularly
      striking and indicates that the six Cl ligands in the distorted
      octahedral embedding push the occupied Ni d-states well below E_F
      -- removing them from the energy range where H-1s hybridization
      would be effective.

(4) TM-3d / Cl-3p MIXING:
    - In panels (g-l), the TM-3d states show stronger spectral overlap
      with the Cl-3p band (which spans ~-6 to -2 eV). This increased
      TM-3d/Cl-3p hybridization is a direct consequence of the additional
      three Cl neighbors (sixfold vs. threefold), and it further
      "buries" TM d-states in bonding combinations that are energetically
      unavailable for H adsorption.

In summary, the conclusion that "embedding reorganizes the TM-3d
manifold" is derived from (i) the redistribution of TM-3d spectral
weight into multiple narrow peaks, (ii) the reduction of TM-3d density
at E_F, (iii) the increased TM-3d/Cl-3p hybridization visible in the
PDOS overlap, and (iv) the cumulative TM-Cl ICOHP values that jump
from ~-6.7 eV (threefold) to ~-11.2 eV (sixfold) for Co.

MANUSCRIPT CHANGE: We have substantially expanded the paragraph

(2) E_F density reduction, and (3) Cl-3p/TM-3d overlap increase as the
three observable spectral signatures of d-manifold reorganization.
This makes the conclusion directly verifiable from the figure.
