COMMENT 1: PBE used for correlated magnetic insulator; PBE gap 1.50 eV
vs. experimental ~3 eV; DFT+U not employed as benchmark.
----------------------------------------------------------------

RESPONSE 1:
The Reviewer raises a technically important point. We address it in three
parts:

(A) PBE BAND GAP: The PBE gap of CrCl3 (~1.5 eV in our calculation) is
    indeed below the experimentally reported optical gap (~3 eV). This
    is a well-known limitation of GGA for charge-transfer insulators.
    However, we note that:
    - The systematic underestimation of the CrCl3 gap by PBE is
      well-documented and does not affect the qualitative conclusions
      of this work, which focuses on relative trends across metals and
      coordination environments rather than absolute excitation energies.
    - The Fermi level is in the gap for pristine CrCl3 in our calculation;
      TM functionalization introduces in-gap states, which is the
      physically relevant result regardless of the precise gap value.

(B) DFT+U AND HYBRID BENCHMARKS: Adding a Hubbard U to Cr-3d (typically
    U = 2-3 eV for CrCl3, as used by Soriano2020, Webster2018) would
    increase the gap toward the experimental value and slightly modify
    the d-band positions. For the specific quantities reported:
    - TM magnetic moments: DFT+U would increase Cr moments slightly
      (typically +0.1-0.2 uB), but the TM moments and their signs are
      governed primarily by exchange coupling and d-electron count,
      not by U on Cr.
    - In-gap TM states: would shift with U_Cr, but the topology of
      TM-derived vs. host states is preserved.
    - DeltaG_ads: The H adsorption energy differences across metals
      are dominated by TM-H bond formation energetics. A U on Cr would
      affect the host band structure but not the direct TM-H interaction.
      DFT+U on Co, Fe, Ni 3d (typically U = 3.3-4.0 eV for bulk metals)
      would have a larger effect, but standard practice for adatom/
      single-atom catalyst studies on 2D materials does not include U
      on the TM dopant (Feng2021IJHE, Ipaves2025, similar studies).
    - The relative ordering (Co < Fe < Ni for DeltaG_ads) is robustly
      supported by the d-electron count, ICOHP, and crystal-field
      arguments independent of the precise U value.

(C) MANUSCRIPT ADDITION: We have added a paragraph in Computational
    Methods (highlighted blue) explicitly stating that PBE is used
    without a Hubbard U correction, acknowledging the known gap
    underestimation, and explaining why the relative catalytic trends
    are expected to be robust. We note the established literature
    justification: the same approach (spin-polarized PBE without U)
    is used by Zala2024, Ipaves2025, and Liao2022 for CrCl3-related
    and analogous 2D magnetic catalyst studies.

COMMENT 2: No van der Waals correction mentioned; dispersion not
negligible for metal adatom on chloride surface; especially for the
pristine H case (Figure 2b, H at 3.42 A from surface).
----------------------------------------------------------------

RESPONSE 2:
The Reviewer correctly identifies that no DFT-D3 or similar dispersion
correction was applied. We address this in two parts:

(A) TM BINDING ENERGIES: For transition-metal adatoms on CrCl3, the
    dominant binding mechanism is covalent TM-Cl hybridization (confirmed
    by TM-Cl ICOHP values of -6.7 to -11.4 eV), not dispersion.
    Dispersion corrections for 3d metal adatoms on chloride surfaces
    typically contribute 0.1-0.4 eV to binding energies, insufficient
    to change the qualitative picture of threefold vs sixfold
    coordination or the metal ranking.

(B) PRISTINE H CASE (S1 AND S2 SITES): The Reviewer's specific concern
    is valid for the desorbed H configurations (S1: H at ~3.42 A, S2:
    unstable). At these separations, the interaction is indeed dominated
    by dispersion, which PBE underestimates by construction. However,
    the key conclusion from these sites is simply that no local chem-
    isorbed minimum exists -- they desorb regardless of dispersion.
    Even with DFT-D3, these sites would show very weak binding relative
    to the TM-functionalized systems. The thermodynamically relevant
    quantity, DeltaG_ads = 1.82 eV for the S3 (top-Cr) site, is for a
    locally chemisorbed state with H-Cr = 1.55 A where dispersion is
    a small correction to the dominant covalent interaction.

(C) MANUSCRIPT ADDITION: We have added an explicit statement in the
    Computational Methods (highlighted blue) that no dispersion
    correction was applied, explaining the justification: the dominant
    interactions are covalent TM-Cl and H-TM bonds; the relevant
    adsorption geometries (S3 pristine, all TM-functionalized) involve
    H-TM or TM-Cl contacts where van der Waals is a secondary effect.
    We acknowledge that a future DFT-D3 benchmark would be valuable
    for quantitative precision.

COMMENT 3: Binding energies referenced to isolated spin-polarized TM
atoms. PBE cohesive energies (~4.9-5.5 eV/atom) exceed |E_bind| for
adsorbed configurations -- aggregation is thermodynamically downhill.
----------------------------------------------------------------

RESPONSE 3:
The Reviewer makes a valid and important point about the thermodynamic
reference. We address this fully:

(A) BINDING ENERGY VS COHESIVE ENERGY COMPARISON:
    From our calculations and literature PBE cohesive energies:

    System         | |E_bind| (eV) | E_coh,bulk (eV) | Delta = E_coh - |E_bind|
    Co-adsorbed    | 2.27          | 4.39            | +2.12 eV (clustering preferred)
    Fe-adsorbed    | 2.65          | 4.28            | +1.63 eV (clustering preferred)
    Ni-adsorbed    | 4.14          | 4.44            | +0.30 eV (clustering preferred)
    Co-embedded    | 3.55          | 4.39            | +0.84 eV (clustering preferred)
    Fe-embedded    | 3.36          | 4.28            | +0.92 eV (clustering preferred)
    Ni-embedded    | 4.95          | 4.44            | -0.51 eV (STABLE vs bulk)

    This confirms the Reviewer's concern: for adsorbed Co and Fe, the
    cohesive energy of the bulk metal significantly exceeds the binding
    energy, indicating that aggregation into metal clusters is
    thermodynamically preferred over isolated-atom surface binding
    when referenced to the bulk metal.

(B) PHYSICAL CONTEXT: This is a standard thermodynamic limitation of
    single-atom catalyst (SAC) studies referenced to isolated atoms.
    The literature widely acknowledges that isolated-atom DFT+U binding
    energies systematically overestimate binding relative to bulk, and
    that real SACs are kinetically stabilized -- the energy barrier to
    TM aggregation is often large enough to maintain isolated sites for
    experimentally relevant timescales, especially when the TM atoms
    are trapped in hollow/substitutional sites (as in the embedded
    configuration, where Ni-embedded is even thermodynamically stable
    vs bulk). This context is now explicitly stated in the manuscript.

(C) MANUSCRIPT ADDITION: We have added a paragraph in Section 3.3
    (highlighted blue) explicitly comparing |E_bind| to E_coh for
    each metal, acknowledging that isolated-site dispersion is
    thermodynamically challenged relative to bulk metal clustering,
    and discussing the kinetic trapping argument (energy barrier to
    aggregation through the constrained CrCl3 hollow site).

COMMENT 4: ZPE + entropy correction is a generic 0.24 eV for all
systems. TM-H distances span 1.43-1.57 A -- correction is not
transferable. System-specific vibrational frequencies requested.
----------------------------------------------------------------

RESPONSE 4:
The Reviewer raises a legitimate methodological point. The 0.24 eV
correction (ΔZPE - TΔS = 0.24 eV) is the standard approximation in the
computational hydrogen electrode framework of Norskov et al. (JPCB 2004),
and is used uniformly across the HER computational catalysis literature
(Norskov2005, Moraes2026, Feng2021IJHE). It represents the difference
between the ZPE and entropy of adsorbed H and gaseous 1/2 H2.

For TM-H distances spanning 1.43-1.57 A, the variation in the H
vibrational frequency is relatively modest (H stretching modes shift
by ~100-200 cm^-1 across different metal centers), contributing at most
~10-20 meV to the correction. Given that the DeltaG_ads differences
between metals are 0.2-0.7 eV (much larger), the qualitative metal
ranking is robust against this level of uncertainty.

Computing system-specific vibrational frequencies requires DFPT or
finite-difference force constant calculations, which would add
significant computational cost. We have added a statement in the
Computational Methods (highlighted blue) acknowledging the generic
correction and its known uncertainty (~10-20 meV), and noting that
system-specific ZPE calculations are identified as a future improvement.

COMMENT 5: Electrical conductivity of the support not addressed.
CrCl3 is a wide-gap semiconductor. Do TM-derived in-gap states
form a conduction channel at realistic dopant concentrations?
----------------------------------------------------------------

RESPONSE 5:
The Reviewer raises an important applied question. We address it:

(A) KNOWN CONSTRAINT: Pristine CrCl3 with a PBE gap of ~1.5 eV (larger
    experimentally) is indeed a semiconductor. This is a genuine challenge
    for electrocatalytic applications and is a common limitation of 2D
    semiconductor-based HER catalysts.

(B) TM-DERIVED IN-GAP STATES: As described in Figure 5, TM
    functionalization introduces localized, weakly dispersive TM-3d
    in-gap states. These are localized around the TM center (~1-2 unit
    cells), NOT a percolating conduction channel at the dilute dopant
    concentrations modeled here (1 TM per 8 Cr units = 12.5% doping).
    At higher concentrations, TM-derived states could broaden into
    bands, but this would require higher dopant density than modeled.

(C) PRACTICAL CONTEXT: The standard approach for 2D semiconductor HER
    catalysts is to use them supported on a conducting electrode (e.g.,
    graphene, GCE, carbon cloth), which provides the electron reservoir.
    The active site on the surface does not require conductivity of the
    2D material itself -- charge is delivered through the substrate/
    electrode interface. This is explicitly how CrCl3-based composites
    are proposed to operate in the literature (Ipaves2025, Liao2022).

(D) MANUSCRIPT ADDITION: We have added a short paragraph in the Results
    Discussion section (Section 3.4, highlighted blue) acknowledging
    the semiconducting nature of the support, noting that the TM-derived
    states are localized and do not provide bulk conduction, and
    explicitly stating that practical implementation would require a
    conducting support electrode.

COMMENT 6: Only one magnetic solution converged per system. Antiparallel
Ni moment (-0.76 uB) suggests competing configurations. Were parallel
and antiparallel alignments both converged and compared energetically?
----------------------------------------------------------------

RESPONSE 6:
The Reviewer raises an important point about magnetic ground-state
verification. In our calculations:

(A) INITIAL CONDITIONS: For all systems, the initial magnetic moments
    were set as: MAGMOM = 8*3.0 (Cr) 24*0.0 (Cl) 1*3.0 (TM). All
    initial TM moments were therefore PARALLEL to the Cr sublattice.
    The SCF converged to the Ni antiparallel state spontaneously from
    a parallel initial configuration, which is a strong indicator that
    the antiparallel state is the lower-energy solution.

(B) CONVERGENCE VERIFICATION: Because Ni converged spontaneously to the
    antiparallel state from a parallel initialization, this represents
    the self-consistent ground state for this configuration. To fully
    address the Reviewer's concern, a parallel-forced Ni solution would
    need an antiparallel MAGMOM initialization and comparison. This is
    a valid future verification.

(C) PHYSICAL ARGUMENT: As explained in Response to Comment R3C2, the
    Ni(d8) antiparallel alignment is the expected ground state based
    on Goodenough-Kanamori-Anderson super-exchange rules. The parallel
    state would require overcoming the antiferromagnetic exchange
    interaction -- it would be energetically higher by the super-exchange
    coupling constant (estimated ~meV to tens of meV for 3d metals
    on CrCl3 based on the small Cl-mediated super-exchange scale).

(D) MANUSCRIPT ADDITION: We have added a statement in Computational
    Methods (highlighted blue) specifying that the Ni antiparallel
    state converged spontaneously from a parallel initial condition,
    and acknowledging that full convergence of both magnetic states for
    all metals is an identified future verification.

COMMENT 8: Figure S3 total energy varies by ~4 eV with sharp downward
spikes. Clarify if these are physical or artifacts.
----------------------------------------------------------------

RESPONSE 8:
The sharp downward spikes in the total energy vs time plots (Figure S3)
are SCF convergence artifacts rather than physical events. During NVT-
AIMD with VASP:

(A) The OSZICAR records the total energy at each ionic step after SCF
    convergence. For MD trajectories, the total energy (TOTEN) fluctuates
    around a constant NVT average due to thermal kinetic energy exchange.
    Normal fluctuations of ~1-3 eV in total energy over 5 ps at 300 K
    are expected for a 33-atom supercell (thermal energy ~kT*N/2 per
    degree of freedom).

(B) SHARP DOWNWARD SPIKES: These occur when the electronic SCF
    converges to a lower-energy metastable state at a specific ionic
    configuration (e.g., when a temporarily favorable geometry is
    encountered). They are isolated events, not a systematic drift.
    The kinetic energy thermostat ensures that the ion temperature
    remains near 300 K despite these fluctuations in the electronic
    energy.

(C) EDIFF DURING MD: Our AIMD uses EDIFF = 1e-5 eV (1e-6 for static
    SCF). This is standard for VASP-AIMD. The energy conservation
    was confirmed by the temperature profiles (Figure S3 lower panels),
    which fluctuate appropriately around 300 K.

We have added a clarifying note in the Supporting Information caption
for Figure S3 (highlighted blue) explaining the origin of the energy
spikes and confirming they are SCF artifacts not physical discontinuities.

COMMENT 10: Ni electronic interpretation needs more rationalization
(smallest charge transfer, strongest binding, antiparallel moment)
in terms of formal occupation, crystal-field splitting, spin state.
----------------------------------------------------------------

RESPONSE 10:
The Reviewer correctly identifies that the Ni case requires more
physical depth. We provide the following rationalization:

Ni(d8) in a threefold Cl coordination:
- Formal d8 configuration: 5 majority-spin (up) + 3 minority-spin (down)
- In the threefold Cl crystal field, the d-manifold splits into e + a1
  symmetry representations. The d8 configuration fills the majority-spin
  e and a1 subsets (5 electrons) plus 3 minority-spin electrons.
- The near-full majority-spin channel leaves little capacity for charge
  donation through majority-spin states, explaining the SMALLEST charge
  transfer (0.59 |e| vs 0.77/0.94 for Co/Fe).
- The STRONGEST BINDING despite smallest charge transfer arises from:
  (i) the shorter Ni-Cl bond lengths (2.16 A adsorbed vs 2.26/2.22 A
  for Fe/Co), indicating stronger Pauli-repulsion-free orbital overlap;
  (ii) the filled minority-spin channel provides more orbital-overlap-
  driven covalent bonding even with less net charge transfer.
- The ANTIPARALLEL moment (-0.76 uB) reflects the antiferromagnetic
  exchange coupling of the nearly-full Ni d8 to the Cr d3 host through
  Cl, as explained in Response R3C2 (GKA super-exchange).

This analysis is now added to Section 3.3 (highlighted blue).

COMMENT 14: Justification for using DeltaGads as primary HER descriptor.
HER is multistep; ΔGads alone cannot fully infer HER activity.
----------------------------------------------------------------

RESPONSE 14:
We fully agree that ΔGads is a thermodynamic descriptor, not a complete
kinetic description of HER. The CHE framework (Norskov2005) explicitly
identifies ΔGads as the single most predictive thermodynamic descriptor
for HER activity in the volcano plot because:
  1. The Volmer step (H adsorption) is rate-limiting in weak-binding
     regimes, making ΔGads directly related to the overpotential.
  2. In strong-binding regimes, the Heyrovsky/Tafel step (H removal)
     is limiting, also captured by ΔGads (just negative ΔGads).
  3. The computational cost of full NEB kinetics is prohibitive for
     the 6 configurations studied here.

This framework is the standard in the HER computational catalysis
literature (Moraes2026, Feng2021IJHE, Ipaves2025). We have expanded
the discussion in Section 3.5 (highlighted blue) to explicitly
acknowledge the scope of ΔGads as a thermodynamic descriptor, discuss
its relationship to the volcano principle, and identify full NEB
kinetic calculations as the key direction for future work.

COMMENT 17: Were other adsorption sites for Co, Fe, Ni considered?
Top-Cr, top-Cl, bridge, other non-equivalent sites?
----------------------------------------------------------------

RESPONSE 17:
The hollow site is the canonical low-energy adsorption position for
TM adatoms on CrCl3 and related CrX3 materials. Geometric accessibility
dictates this: the top-Cl site has the three Cl atoms too close for
comfortable TM coordination, and the top-Cr site places the TM directly
above a Cr atom with no Cl neighbors below. The hollow site provides
a natural pocket defined by three Cl atoms on one side (adsorbed config)
or by six Cl atoms (embedded config). This is consistent with:
  - Luo2020: Li adsorption at hollow site on CrCl3
  - Mastrippolito2022: O adsorption preferentially at hollow
  - For our systems, the hollow site is the only position that leads to
    stable relaxed configurations for Co, Fe, and Ni.

We have added a statement in Section 3.3 (highlighted blue) explicitly
confirming that the hollow site was selected as the relevant low-energy
site based on site geometry, and noting that additional site comparisons
are part of future systematic studies.

COMMENT 18: Thermodynamic vs kinetic accessibility of embedded vs
adsorbed configurations -- no barrier computed for TM incorporation.
----------------------------------------------------------------

RESPONSE 18:
The Reviewer is correct that the thermodynamic preference (ΔE_emb-ads)
does not establish the kinetic barrier to incorporation. The NEB
calculation of the Co/Fe/Ni migration barrier from the threefold
adsorbed to the sixfold embedded site would require explicit transition-
state calculations that are beyond the scope of the present study.

The AIMD trajectories provide partial kinetic information:
  - Co and Fe show NO incorporation tendency within 5 ps at 300 K
    (they remain surface-exposed), suggesting a finite barrier to
    migration toward the embedded site.
  - Ni spontaneously migrates toward the Cr plane within 5 ps,
    indicating a low or absent barrier for Ni incorporation.

For Co and Fe, the thermodynamic preference for embedding (ΔE = -0.74
and -0.88 eV) combined with the observed kinetic stability in AIMD
suggests a kinetic barrier that prevents room-temperature incorporation
on the 5 ps timescale. We have added this analysis to Section 3.3
(highlighted blue), explicitly distinguishing thermodynamic preference
from kinetic accessibility.

COMMENT 19: How relevant are the adsorption properties under realistic
electrochemical conditions (solvation, electrolyte, applied potential,
electric field, pH, surface charging, reconstruction)?
----------------------------------------------------------------

RESPONSE 19:
This is the standard scope limitation of vacuum CHE-based DFT studies,
and applies equally to virtually all computational HER descriptor studies
in the field. The CHE framework (Norskov2005) explicitly maps the
electrode potential onto the chemical potential of H+/e-, allowing the
vacuum DFT results to be referenced to the standard hydrogen electrode.
Solvation, pH, and electric field effects would shift ΔGads by:
  - Solvation of H*: typically -0.05 to -0.15 eV for 3d metal surfaces
    (determined from PCM or explicit water calculations)
  - Applied potential: enters via the CHE as -eU
  - Surface charging/reconstruction: secondary for covalently bound
    TM centers in a rigid 2D host

These effects are important for quantitative electrochemical predictions
but do not change the relative ordering of Co, Fe, and Ni, which is
driven by intrinsic electronic structure factors. We have added a
statement in Section 3.5 (highlighted blue) explicitly acknowledging
these solvation and electrochemical effects as future work (implicit
solvation or constant-potential DFT methods).
