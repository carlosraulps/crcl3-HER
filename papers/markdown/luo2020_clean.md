### A B S T R A C T

Two-dimensional (2D) CrCl3 has attracted much attention as it is reported to be a ferromagnetic semiconductor. By performing first-principles calculations, we investigate the effects of Li and F adsorption on the saturation magnetization and magnetic anisotropy energy (MAE) of CrCl3. It is observed that Li adsorption can dramatically enhance its saturation magnetization, and tune its easy magnetization axis to the in-plane direction from original out-of-plane. The monotonic enhancement of in-plane magnetism in CrCl3 as the coverage of Li increases are attributed to electrostatic doping induced by charge transfer between Li atoms and Br atoms. By contrast, the F adsorption reduces the out-of-plane magnetism in CrCl3 as the coverage of F increases but keeps the original easy magnetization. These findings will benefit for further understanding of the tunable magnetic properties of CrCl3 and push 2D spintronics applications. 1. Introduction Recent discoveries of two-dimensional (2D) materials have launched great concern theoretically and experimentally because of their unique electronic and optoelectronic properties [1–5]. However, none of con­ ventional 2D materials is reported to exhibit intrinsic magnetism, which limits their application in spintronics [6]. Large numbers of experi­ mental and theoretical have been carried out on introducing magnetism into nonmagnetic 2D materials or seeking 2D materials with intrinsic magnetism. On the one hand, the general methods of inducing magne­ tism in nonmagnetic 2D materials were substitutional doping [7,8], defect engineering [9], magnetic proximity effect [10], and so on. Although some achievements have been made by these approaches, the introduced magnetism is relatively weak and difficult to control exper­ imentally. On the other hand, a variety of theoretical prediction about new 2D ferromagnetic materials, such as CrI3 [11,12], Cr2Ge2Te6 [13], VSe2 [14], MnSex [15], and Fe3GeTe2 [16], Fe5GeTe2 [17], have been reported. These findings provide new exciting opportunities for future spintronic applications. Magnetic anisotropy energy (MAE) plays a key role in 2D magnetic materials, and it could overcome the thermal fluctuations and stabilizes the magnetic order at the finite temperature which has been verified by the Mermin–Wagner theorem [18]. Moreover, ferromagnetic 2D mate­ rials with large anisotropy come to attract growing attention for their potential applications in spintronic devices. It is especially attractive to explore whether intrinsic ferromagnetism and the magnetic anisotropy of 2D monolayer can be modulated. Previous works indicated that sur­ face adsorption is an attractive approach to control electronic structure and magnetism in 2Dmaterials [19]. By adsorption, a charge transfer can be induced between adatoms and the neighbor atoms of host materials, leading to electrostatic doping. As is well known, Li is a typical electron donor, likely transfers charge to the substrate when adsorbed. More recently, many works have been carried out to investigate the MAE in intrinsic magnetic materials. Richter et al. observed temperature-dependent magnetic anisotropy in CrI3 and CrBr3 [20]. The magnetic anisotropy in monolayer CrCl3, CrBr3, and CrI3 could be regulated by the strain [21]. Kim et al. studied the magnetic coupling in 2D-CrX3 (X = I, Br, Cl) by using the combination of tunneling and magneto-optical measurements [22]. Yang et al. reported nonmetallic atoms induced magnetic anisotropy in monolayer CrBr3 and CrI3 [23]. As one of the intrinsic magnetic materials, the study of CrCl3 is still limited. CrCl3, CrBr3, and CrI3 have several similarities, such as FM and insulating properties. Method All calculations were carried out in the framework of density func­ tional theory (DFT) within the generalized gradient approximation (GGA) of Perdew-Burke-Ernzerhof (PBE) [24] implemented in the Vienna ab initio simulation package

### (VASP)

[25]. The projector-augmented wave (PAW) [26] method was used to describe the ion-electron interaction. The plane-wave cut-off energy was set to be 500 eV. The Brillouin zone was sampled by using a 10 × 10 × 1-centered Monkhorst–Pack k-point grid for the unit cell and 5 × 5 × 1 k-point for 3 × 2 × 1 supercell. All the atomic structures were fully relaxed until the force on each atom is smaller than 0.01 eV/Ǻ and the total energy was converged to be within at least 10−6 eV (10−8 eV for MAE calculations). The optimized lattice constants are a = b = 6.056 Å, and the Cr–Cl bond length is about 2.352 Å [21]. To avoid the artificial interactions between image layers, a vacuum space of 20 Å is added along with the out of the plane z-axis. Spin-orbit coupling was taken into account to obtain the MAE. Convergence test has been carefully carried out for k-point sam­ pling on MAE. The results show that the values of MAE converge to 10−5 eV/Cr as cut-off energy changes from 450 eV to 550 eV, and k mesh from 5 × 5 × 1 to 10 × 10 × 1 for pristine CrCl3. 3. Results and discussion 3.1. Magnetic properties of pristine CrCl3 In this part, we investigated the structure and magnetism of the pristine CrCl3 monolayer, as shown in Fig. 1(a) and (b). It consists of 2D honeycomb network of Cr atoms, which are sandwiched between two atomic planes of Cl. Moreover, Cr atoms are coordinated by edge- sharing octahedra with six Cl atoms. Our calculation shows that pris­ tine CrCl3 has a direct bandgap of 1.59 eV, which is consistent with the earlier theoretical result [21]. The pristine CrCl3 also shows large magnetism as large as about 3.0 μB/Cr. As shown in Fig. 1(c), from the projected density of states (PDOS), we can see the magnetism is domi­ nated by the hybrid orbitals of Cr-d and Cl-p. As is well known, MAE is an important parameter to characterize FM materials besides the magnetization and TC. In the previous work, it has been found that the magnetic properties of CrI3 monolayer are sensitive to charge doping [27]. Inspired by this, we deem that whether the MAE of CrCl3 could be regulated by charge doping. We calculated the MAE with charge doping by only adding electrons or holes to pristine CrCl3 (see Fig. 2). Here, the MAE is evaluated based on the total energy dif­ ference between in-plane [100] and out-of-plane [001] magnetization direction, named MAE = E[100]-E[001]. Thus, the positive and negative values of MAE denote that the easy magnetization axis is perpendicular and parallel to the plane of monolayer CrCl3, respectively. For the pristine CrCl3, the calculated MAE is about 0.789 meV/Cr, the positive value indicates an out-of-plane easy magnetization axis. The MAE can be largely tuned by charge doping. Magnetic properties of Li-adsorbed CrCl3 First of all, to obtain the most energetically favorable adsorption configuration, we have considered three adsorption sites labeled as (s1) hollow (at the hollow of honeycomb network of Cr atoms), (s2) Cr-top (at the top of Cr atom), and (s3) Cl-top (at the top of Cl atom), as shown in Fig. 1(a) and (b). After fully relaxation, as listed in Table 1, the hollow site configuration has the lowest energy (~3.642 eV), and the Cl- top configuration is the most unfavored (~3.030 eV). Based on the current results, the hollow site configuration is considered as the most stable configuration. The impact of Li coverage on the electronic and magnetic properties of monolayer CrCl3 is investigated. When Li coverage changes from 0% to 12.5%, as shown in Fig. 3(a), the magnetism is almost linearly enhanced as the Li coverage increases, along with the local spin moment of the Cr. It is clear that the total magnetic moment is mainly contributed by Cr atoms, with 3.5 μB for each Cr at the 12.5% Li-coverage, much higher than that of the pristine one (~3.0 μB). Besides, the total energy difference between the stable AFM and FM configuration is shown in Fig. 3(b). It is found that the energy difference (EAFM-EFM) is becoming much larger as the Li- coverage increases. As Li-coverage increases from 0% to 12.5%, it changes from 27 meV to 181 meV. The 0% coverage means the pristine CrCl3 monolayer. Our results indicate the most stable state is always FM, and the FM coupling in CrCl3–Li is enhanced by Li adsorption. These results further confirm that the magnetism can be enhanced after Li adsorption. As we mentioned above, the magnetism is almost linearly enhanced as the Li coverage increases. In our opinion, the enhancement of the magnetism could be understood as below. In every octahedral coordi­ nation, the Cr-d orbitals split into triply degenerated t2g (dxy, dyz, and dxz) and doubly degenerated eg (dz2 and dx2-y2), the former with lower energy. In pristine CrCl3, each coordinated Cr has a 3d3 configuration, and the three t2g orbitals are singly occupied with parallel spin. Whereas in CrCl3–Li, due to an electron doping effect, besides three electrons occupy the three t2g orbitals, the other electrons occupy the higher eg orbital according to Hund’s rules, thus resulting in larger magnetic moment. Also, the charge density and PDOS of one Li- adsorbed CrCl3 are plotted in Fig. 4. As expected, Li atoms provide electrons to CrCl3 monolayer, as shown in Fig. 4(a) and (b). These changes in local structure will influence the electronic structure. As illustrated in Fig. 4(c), the Li-s and Cl-p orbitals are coupled, indirectly altering the hybridization between Cl-p and Cr-d orbitals. Comparing

In current cases, the Li–Cl charge transfer indirectly results in Li atoms donating partial electrons to Cr atoms, which brings about an electron doping effect. These results indicate that charge transfer due to Li adsorption can significantly affect the electronic properties of CrCl3. These results further confirm that the magnetism can be enhanced after Li adsorption. Finally, we investigate how MAE changes with Li coverage. When Li is adsorbed onto CrCl3 monolayer. the MAE values change from positive to negative at certain coverage (between 4.17% and 6.25%) and in­ crease negatively and monotonically with increasing Li adsorption coverage, as shown in Fig. 5. For example, the MAE changes from 0.789 meV/Cr to −2.303 meV/Cr while Li adsorption coverage increases from 0% to 12.5%. These results clearly show that we can effectively switch the easy magnetization orientation from out-of-plane to the in-plane direction by adsorbing Li atoms. We attribute the change of MAE to the electron doping effect induced by charge transfer between Li and Cr atoms. 3.3. Magnetic properties of F-adsorbed CrCl3 The adsorption of F atoms on monolayer CrCl3 is considered to simulate the practical hole doping. Since the electronegativity of F is stronger than Cl, direct charge transfer may occur between F and Cr, thus leading to hole doping. To confirm the most stable adsorption configuration, we also considered three initial adsorption configuration as mentioned above. It is found that F atoms always relax far away from the initial high symmetrical sites, indicating strong re-construction in our DFT calculations. To qualitatively reveal the effect of the hole doping and compare with CrCl3–Li, the MAE of CrCl3–F was also calculated with F atoms fixed at the hollow site as in CrCl3–F.
